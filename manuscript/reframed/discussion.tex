\section{Discussion}

We report that the emergence of drug-tolerant persisters under EGFR
inhibition is accompanied by a genuine, non-Gaussian topological
reorganization of the single-cell transcriptomic state space, and that
this reorganization is detectable only when the relevant null is matched
to the dispersion of the data. In the osimertinib-treated PC9 cell line,
the largest $H_1$ persistence sits at or below a covariance-matched
Gaussian null at baseline (D0 ratio $0.95$, real$>$null in $31\%$ of
bootstrap subsamples) and after early exposure (D3 ratio $0.91$, $18\%$),
but rises above the null once persisters establish (D7 ratio $1.31$,
$100\%$; D14 ratio $1.42$, $98\%$). The within-system directional effect
is the robust claim: osimertinib D7 and D14 each exceed D0 in $100\%$ of
bootstrap pairs. The same pattern recurs in an EGFR-mutant
patient-derived xenograft, where residual disease after treatment carries
markedly more topological structure than untreated tumour (residual ratio
$1.95$, $99\%$; untreated ratio $1.21$, $86\%$), and residual exceeds
untreated in $97\%$ of bootstrap pairs. The methodological contribution is
the dispersion-matched Gaussian null itself: by simulating point clouds
with the same mean and covariance but no genuine structure, it separates
real topological organization from the inflation of $H_1$ persistence that
high-dimensional dispersion alone produces.

\subsection*{What we do not claim}

We explicitly do not claim that cells traverse a cycle, nor that drug
tolerance is a reversible cyclic plasticity. A construct-validity analysis
designed as a negative control argues directly against traversal. When we
estimate circular coordinates on the candidate loop, the angular
distribution collapses onto essentially a single phase in every cohort
(Rayleigh $R \ge 0.965$), far from the dispersed occupancy a traversed
cycle would produce, and far from the $R = 0.881$ of a synthetic control
loop occupied by only $12\%$ of cells. Lineage-barcoded Watermelon clones
are tighter on the candidate loop than random cells, not spread around it.
The excess $H_1$ persistence we measure therefore reflects a
\emph{sparse persistent loop occupied by a minority of cells} as a static
geometric feature of the state space, not a population-wide orbit that
cells move along. Reframing the circular-coordinate result as a negative
control is, in our view, the honest reading: it shows where the topology
is informative (a non-Gaussian feature is present) and where it is silent
(it says nothing about dynamics or reversibility).

\subsection*{Scope and specificity}

The drug-induction signal is specific to the controlled cell-line and PDX
systems treated with osimertinib. Under erlotinib, the same statistic
shows no excess over the dispersion-matched null at any time point (D0,
D9, D11 ratios $0.93$, $0.99$, $0.70$). Rather than a failure, we read
this as specificity evidence: the readout does not manufacture structure
where none is present, and the erlotinib cohort functions as an internal
negative control alongside baseline and the Gaussian null. We are also
deliberately conservative about the patient data. In the Maynard cohort,
excess topological structure is present at \emph{all} disease stages,
including treatment-naive tumours (TN, PER, PD ratios $1.52$, $1.69$,
$1.51$). Because the excess is already there before treatment, it most
plausibly reflects baseline tumour heterogeneity rather than
drug-induced reorganization. We therefore treat the patient cohorts as a
heterogeneity caveat, not as a drug-induction claim, and restrict the
causal reading of treatment-associated reorganization to the osimertinib
cell-line and PDX systems where a matched untreated or baseline contrast
is available.

\subsection*{Limitations}

Several limitations bound the interpretation. First, the feature is
sparse and minority-occupied: it is carried by a small subset of cells,
so it is invisible to population-averaged summaries and demands the
dispersion-matched null to be distinguished from background dispersion at
all. Second, point estimates of max $H_1$ are unstable across subsamples,
which is why we do not rest any claim on a single persistence value;
instead we report bootstrap confidence intervals and base every
conclusion on directional robustness across paired subsamples (the
fraction of bootstrap pairs in which the treated or residual condition
exceeds its control). Third, the patient-tumour confound described above
means our results cannot speak to whether the same reorganization occurs
under therapy in patients; resolving this would require paired
pre-treatment and residual-disease sampling within individuals. Fourth,
the topological statistic characterizes the geometry of the state space
but is agnostic to its mechanistic origin and to its dynamics; it
identifies that a non-Gaussian loop exists and which cells lie on it, but
not why those cells occupy it or whether that occupancy changes over time.

\subsection*{A generalizable readout}

The central transferable element is the dispersion-controlled null. Much
of the appeal and much of the difficulty of applying persistent homology
to single-cell data stems from the fact that $H_1$ persistence grows with
the ambient dispersion and dimensionality of a point cloud even in the
complete absence of organized structure. A covariance-matched Gaussian
null absorbs exactly that nuisance variation, so that any residual excess
is attributable to genuine non-Gaussian geometry. Paired with bootstrap
directional testing for stability, this yields a readout that reports not
"is there a loop" in absolute terms, but "is there more loop-like
structure than dispersion alone would create, and does that excess move
reproducibly with a perturbation." This framing is portable to other
perturbation-response settings in single-cell biology, and it positions
the work as a method-and-observation contribution: a dispersion-controlled
topological statistic, validated on a drug-tolerance system, with explicit
construct-validity and specificity controls that guard against the
overinterpretation such statistics readily invite.
